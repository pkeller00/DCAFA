voomCLR in CA1.py: original Python approximation vs R-matched mode (voom_match_r=True)
=========================================================================================

*** 2026-10-17: the original Python approximation has been REMOVED from CA1.py. ***
backend="voomclr" now always runs the R-matched version and the voom_match_r switch is
gone (rows are tagged coding="voomclr_mode_r"). This file documents how results saved
before that date (coding="voomclr_mode") differ from the current ones.
Written 2026-10-17.

Both versions share the same pipeline:
  CLR transform -> unweighted fit for fitted counts -> voom precision weights
  -> weighted fit per community -> subtract the modal bias -> eBayes moderation.

They differ in four places. voom_match_r=True switches all four to the behaviour of
official R voomCLR 0.99.41, for both voom_var_calc="analytical" and "empirical".
(Updated 2026-10-17: the empirical trend (section 1b) and the df_prior = Inf case of
eBayes were added.)


1. NB dispersion (used to build the precision weights)
------------------------------------------------------
Original approximation (_voomclr_estimate_nb_dispersion):
  - Fits a Poisson GLM to the raw counts of each community separately, with no
    library-size offset.
  - Takes a method-of-moments estimate:  phi = sum[(y - mu)^2 - mu] / sum(mu^2).
  - Each community is estimated alone; the estimate is clipped at 1e-8.

R-matched (edgeR, now ported to Python in edger_disp.py; no R needed):
  - Uses TMM-normalised library sizes as offsets.
  - Fits NB GLMs and maximises a Cox-Reid adjusted likelihood over a dispersion grid.
  - Fits a trend of dispersion against abundance (locfit).
  - Shrinks each community's dispersion towards that trend by an empirical-Bayes amount
    (limma squeezeVar).

Effect:
  On Fudan io23, edgeR gave up to about twice the moment estimate (c_3: 1.71 vs 0.98).
  The weight is 1 / [((K-1)/K)^2 * (1/mu + phi)], so a larger phi lowers the weights and
  makes them more equal across bags. That flows into every coefficient and SE.


1b. Empirical mean-variance trend (only when voom_var_calc="empirical")
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Original approximation:
  - statsmodels lowess (3 robustness iterations, delta = 0) of sqrt(residual SD) against
    mean log abundance, over all communities.

R-matched (voomCLR, ported in edger_disp.py: r_lowess, r_approxfun_rule2):
  - R's stats::lowess (clowess): 3 robustness iterations, delta = 0.01 * range(x), so
    points closer than delta are interpolated rather than fitted.
  - Communities with a zero total are left out of the trend.
  - The trend is read off with approxfun(rule=2, ties=mean). Weight = 1 / trend^4.

Effect:
  The old empirical path differed from R by up to 1.7 in coefficients on random test
  data. With K = 5 communities the trend runs through only 5 points, so small
  implementation details move it a lot.


2. Bias (mode) correction
-------------------------
Original:
  - The global maximum of a scipy gaussian_kde (Scott's bandwidth), searched on a
    4096-point grid.

R-matched:
  - modeest::mlv(method="meanshift"). It starts at the shorth (the mean of the densest
    half of the values) and climbs to the nearest density peak.
  - It uses bw.nrd0, which is narrower than Scott's because it takes the smaller of the
    SD and IQR/1.34. It can stop at a local peak rather than the global one.

Effect:
  The bias is subtracted from every community's coefficient for that term, so a
  different mode shifts all effects by the same amount. That shift was about 0.1 on log
  scale on Fudan io23.


3. Empirical-Bayes variance moderation
--------------------------------------
Prior variance (s2_prior):
  - The original stops at exp(mean(log s^2 + logmdigamma(df_resid/2))).
  - limma's fitFDist also divides by exp(logmdigamma(df_prior/2)),
    where logmdigamma(x) = log(x) - digamma(x).
  - The original overstates s2_prior when df_prior is small (6x on the voomCLR
    vignette, where df_prior was 0.7). That inflates the moderated SEs and makes the
    original more conservative.

When df_prior = Inf (no spread beyond sampling noise):
  - limma sets s2_prior = mean(s^2), with s^2 floored at 1e-5 * median.
  - The original used the geometric form exp(mean log s^2 + correction), which is larger.
    This changed t by up to 0.07 on random test data.
  - R-matched now follows limma squeezeVar/fitFDist line by line.

Total df:
  - limma caps df_total at the pooled residual df, sum(df_resid). The original doesn't
    cap it.
  - This matters when df_prior is very large or infinite. The original then uses a
    nearly normal reference distribution, and R a capped t, so R's p-values and CIs are
    slightly wider.


In practice
-----------
  voom_match_r=False  keeps earlier voomCLR results reproducible.
                      Rows are tagged coding="voomclr_mode".
  voom_match_r=True   reproduces official R voomCLR 0.99.41 (to about 1e-8 on Fudan io23).
                      Rows are tagged coding="voomclr_mode_r".

The dispersion step causes the largest differences in weights and SEs. The mode step
causes the uniform shift in effect sizes.


Note on the ports (edger_disp.py)
---------------------------------
  - Line-by-line port of edgeR 4.10.6, limma 3.68.5, locfit 1.5-9.12 and R stats::lowess
    for the arguments voomCLR uses.
  - Full pipeline vs the R voomCLR package on 30 random datasets: coefficients, t and p
    agree to within about 1e-7 for both analytical and empirical. Fudan io23 reproduces
    R's output to every printed digit for both settings, with and without age + sex.
  - Checked against R on 46 synthetic datasets (continuous and one-way designs, all-zero
    groups, 2-120 communities). Tagwise dispersions agree to within 4e-12 relative error.
  - End-to-end fit_ca1_multi_targets(backend="voomclr", voom_match_r=True) agrees with the
    previous Rscript-based version to 4e-14.
  - CA1.py imports from edger_disp.py, so keep the two files in the same folder.
